    Pilot guardrails

    1. Start with 2 reps for 60–90 daysChoose one repeatable workflow, not a broad “AI transformation.”
    2. Require 9.0 hours returned per rep each weekMeasure completed work and usable time, not prompts or logins.
    3. Prove at least $164K in annualized revenue liftThat is the break-even lift at your margin and year-one investment.

    How the model works

    Assist case: baseline revenue × automatable share of the workweek × agent automation × productivity capture × adoption. Gross margin converts the revenue lift to gross profit.

    Capacity case: rep count × automatable share × agent automation × adoption. That FTE-equivalent capacity is valued at loaded rep cost.

    ROI: (benefit − recurring cost − rollout cost) ÷ (recurring cost + rollout cost).

    This is a decision model, not a forecast. The two cases are alternative value lenses and should not be added together. Capacity only becomes savings when hiring is avoided, workload is redeployed, or headcount changes. Revenue lift assumes recovered time produces at the team’s current rate.

      How the model works

      Your baseline funnel runs touches → replies → meetings → opportunities → closed-won, per rep, per year. Each AI lever lifts its own stage of that funnel, discounted by the realization rate, and the stages compound — more touches, each converting better, is how the meeting count multiplies.

      Net gain equals incremental gross profit across the team minus year-one AI cost (per-rep seats plus the platform fee). Nobody leaves the model: the output is more pipeline from the same headcount.

      What the math does not capture
      • Deliverability ceilings — inboxes punish raw volume; that is what the realization dial is for
      • AE capacity: 51% more meetings needs someone to take them
      • Data quality, territory saturation, and conversation quality
      • Rep adoption — an uplift nobody uses realizes at 0%
